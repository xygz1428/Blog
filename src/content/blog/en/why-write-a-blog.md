When I started this blog, someone asked me: Who still reads blogs now?

My answer is not "how many readers", but: **Writing a blog is first and foremost for myself**.

## Writing exposes ambiguity

When you think about something in your mind, you feel it is clear.

Once it's time to write it down, the question arises:

- What is the subject of this sentence?
- Is this causal relationship established?
- Are these two concepts the same thing?

**If you can't write, it's usually not a problem with your ability to express yourself, but because you haven't thought through the idea clearly. **

Writing forces you to change "feeling you understand" into "being able to explain clearly". The distance between the two is much greater than imagined.

## a specific example

I originally thought that "static sites do not need a database" and I completely understood this.

It wasn't until I was about to write an article to explain it that I discovered that there was a link that wasn't right: **Why can blog queries be calculated well during construction, but other systems can't? **

After forcing myself to answer this question, I clarified the key point - "Read more and write less, and the results of reading can be calculated in advance." This sentence did not exist in my mind before I wrote it.

## Three by-products of writing

**1. Retrievable Memory**

If you forget a certain conclusion three months later, it’s much faster to search your own blog than to reason again.

**2. Traces of judgment**

Why you made the choice you did and what plan you gave up - these seemed obvious at the time of the decision, but I couldn't remember them at all afterwards. Only when you write it down will you know whether you have made progress.

**3. Unexpected connection**

When writing A, B comes to mind because the two have a common structure. Such connections do not occur spontaneously in the brain.

## About "No one is watching"

If the value of writing comes from readers, then writing without anyone reading means writing in vain.

But its main value lies in the writing process:

| link | income |
|---|---|
| Think clearly | Cognitive benefits (maximum) |
| write it down | memory and retrieval |
| send out | Possible exchanges (extra) |

Publishing only gives the third item a chance to happen, not a prerequisite for writing.

## Why not keep a diary

The diary is written for oneself, allowing blurring and jumping.

Blogging is writing that assumes there is an audience—even if there isn’t one. This assumption forces you to speak in full:

- Don’t use abbreviations that only you understand
- Fill in the skipped reasoning steps
-Explain why this is important

**"Assume there are readers" is a lever to improve the quality of expression. **

## The real cost of writing

A 1,500-word article takes me about two hours:

- Figure out the structure: 40 minutes
- Writing first draft: 50 minutes
- Edited: 30 minutes

Those 30 minutes of editing are the most important – usually 30% of the first draft is crap.

## a suggestion

If you want to write too, start with these three items:

1. **Write about a problem you have really solved recently** (please provide details, no need to make it up)
2. **Assume the reader is himself three months later** (knows the background, but forgets the details)
3. **Write first and then revise** (If you revise while writing, you will get stuck)

Don't wait until you "think clearly before writing" - the process of writing is the process of thinking clearly**.

## summary

Writing is not about recording ideas, it is about creating ideas.

Vague intuition is forced to be precise when writing, and this is its greatest value. Whether or not there are readers is another matter.
